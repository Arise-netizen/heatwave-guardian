1. The Core Thermal Stress Index (Liljegren WBGT)

ISO 7243 Standard

Traditional weather reports emphasize ambient dry-bulb temperature ($T_a$). However, human thermoregulation relies predominantly on the evaporative cooling of sweat. When relative humidity is elevated or direct sunlight is intense, sweat cannot evaporate effectively, causing core body temperatures to rise dangerously even at seemingly moderate ambient temperatures (e.g., 34°C with 80% RH).

The Thermal Stress Index is directly derived from the outdoor Wet-Bulb Globe Temperature (WBGT), which incorporates all four environmental determinants of human thermal load:

$$\text{WBGT}_{\text{outdoor}} = 0.7 \times T_{\text{nwb}} + 0.2 \times T_g + 0.1 \times T_a$$
Natural Wet-Bulb ($T_{\text{nwb}}$) Measures maximum evaporative potential via Stull (2011) psychrometric regression.
Black Globe Temp ($T_g$) Measures radiative solar loading & wind cooling via Liljegren et al. (2008) energy balance.
Ambient Air Temp ($T_a$) Standard dry-bulb shade temperature measured at 2 meters.

2. Why IMD Regional Criteria?

India's geographic diversity makes a single national temperature threshold medically ineffective. A temperature of 38°C is normal in the Thar Desert (Rajasthan), but would be catastrophic in Shimla (Himachal Pradesh).

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) defines heatwave departure criteria across three distinct geographic domains:

Plains Region (≥ 40.0 °C) Heat Day: 40.0–41.9 °C
Heatwave: 42.0–43.9 °C
Severe Heatwave: ≥ 44.0 °C
Coastal Region (≥ 37.0 °C) Heat Day: 37.0–38.9 °C
Heatwave: 39.0–40.9 °C
Severe Heatwave: ≥ 41.0 °C
Hilly Region (≥ 30.0 °C) Heat Day: 30.0–31.9 °C
Heatwave: 32.0–33.9 °C
Severe Heatwave: ≥ 34.0 °C

3. Why Discrete Fusion Matrix instead of Summation?

Both WBGT and IMD alerts derive from correlated atmospheric temperature signals. If an algorithm simply adds their numeric scores together, high temperatures are double-counted, creating spurious extreme alerts.

HeatWave Guardian solves this using a rigorous 20-cell lookup fusion matrix ($5 \times 4$), ensuring each biometeorological condition produces a stable, validated environmental base risk (Low, Moderate, High, Very High, Extreme).

4. Physiological Vulnerability & Escalation

Environmental base risk is transformed into personalized risk through individual physiological factors:

Age Vulnerability Children (<18) have underdeveloped sweat gland density and higher surface-area-to-mass ratios. Seniors (60+) experience reduced cardiovascular stroke volume and diminished thirst perception.
Work Intensity & Metabolic Heat Heavy and very heavy manual labor generates 400–600 Watts of internal metabolic heat, dramatically accelerating the rate of core body temperature rise.
Thermal Adaptation (Experience) Workers unaccustomed to working in hot weather produce less sweat with higher salt loss, triggering circulatory collapse much faster than acclimatized workers.
Clothing & PPE Insulation Heavy overalls and impermeable personal protective gear (PPE) create a vapor barrier that traps metabolic heat and prevents sweat evaporation.

Documented Scientific Limitations

  • Internet Connectivity for Real-Time Weather: Live assessments require active HTTPS connections to Open-Meteo. The system returns HTTP 502 with clean notices rather than fabricating synthetic data when offline.
  • ERA5 Historical Reanalysis Resolution: Coarse ~25km reanalysis grid cells have a published cold bias over India and average out localized ground-station maximums by ~2–4°C.
  • Proxy Indicators are Not Medical Diagnoses: The Experimental Mortality Risk Index is an aggregate decision-support indicator and does not estimate individual clinical outcomes.
